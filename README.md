# GDP and Final Energy Consumption in Argentina (1970–2025)

### Energy Economics | Statistical Analysis | Energy Analytics

**Author:** Gustavo Alfredo Herrero  
**Study area:** Argentina  
**Period:** 1970–2025  
**Data:** 56 annual observations  

## 1. Project Overview

This project examines the relationship between real Gross Domestic Product (GDP) and Final Energy Consumption (FEC) in Argentina over the period 1970–2025.

The study combines energy economics, exploratory data analysis, and statistical modeling to assess how changes in energy consumption are associated with changes in economic activity.

## 2. Context

Energy is an essential input for economic activity, including industry, transportation, commerce, services, and households.

Argentina's economic history provides a relevant context for evaluating the relationship between energy consumption and economic growth across periods of expansion, recession, and structural change.

## 3. Objectives

- Analyze the historical evolution of real GDP and FEC.
- Evaluate the statistical association between their annual growth rates.
- Estimate a simple linear regression model.
- Examine energy intensity and its historical evolution.
- Interpret the results from an energy economics perspective.

## 4. Data and Methodology

**Main data sources:**
- World Bank — World Development Indicators (real GDP).
- Argentina's National Energy Secretariat — National Energy Balances (FEC).

**Methodological approach:**
- Exploratory analysis of annual time series.
- Indexed GDP and FEC comparisons (1970 = 100).
- Year-over-year percentage changes.
- Pearson correlation analysis.
- Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) simple linear regression.
- Residual diagnostics and statistical interpretation.

The main regression uses annual GDP growth as the dependent variable and annual FEC growth as the explanatory variable.

Annual growth rates are emphasized to reduce the risk of misleading associations caused by shared long-term trends.

## 5. Key Findings

- **Pearson correlation:** r = 0.823.
- **Regression coefficient:** β ≈ 1.11.
- **Coefficient of determination:** R² = 0.677.
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001.
- **Regression sample:** 55 annual growth-rate observations.

A 1% increase in FEC is associated, on average, with approximately 1.11% higher real GDP within the estimated model.

The results indicate a strong and statistically significant positive relationship between annual changes in energy consumption and economic activity.

**Important limitation:** The analysis identifies statistical association, not causality. Economic activity depends on multiple factors beyond energy consumption.
